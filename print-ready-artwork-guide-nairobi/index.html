How to prepare artwork for professional printing

A good print starts with a correctly prepared file. Use this checklist before ordering business cards, flyers, posters, banners, stickers or branded T-shirts. It helps reduce avoidable layout problems and makes it easier for the print team to confirm your job.

1. Confirm the finished size first

Set up the document at the final size requested for the product. A business card, A5 flyer, shop poster and roll-up banner all need different page dimensions. Do not resize a small design to a large banner at the last minute: photographs and raster artwork may become visibly soft or pixelated.

For banners and other large-format products, confirm the finished dimensions and orientation before designing. Also consider where the item will be displayed and how much distance people will have when reading it.

2. Use clear, high-resolution images

For photographic images, aim for approximately 300 pixels per inch at the final printed size for small-format work. Large-format artwork may use a different effective resolution depending on viewing distance, so confirm the requirement for the product rather than enlarging a low-resolution image.

  • Use the original photograph or image file where possible.
  • Avoid screenshots, images saved from messaging apps, and tiny social-media downloads for large prints.
  • Use vector artwork for logos and simple illustrations when available. Vector files can scale cleanly without becoming pixelated.
  • Check that logos, photos and other placed artwork are not stretched or distorted.

3. Check colour before exporting

Digital screens normally display RGB colour, while many print workflows use CMYK or other print-specific colour profiles. Bright screen colours can therefore look different when printed. If a particular brand colour matters, supply its colour reference and ask the team to confirm how it can be reproduced on the chosen material.

Do not judge the final print colour from a phone screen alone. A screen preview is useful for layout, but it is not a guarantee of the exact printed shade.

4. Allow for bleed and safe margins

Bleed is extra artwork that extends beyond the final trim edge so a print can be cut without leaving an unintended white line. The safe area is the space inside the trim where important text, logos and contact details should remain.

Important: Bleed and safe-area requirements depend on the product and finishing method. Use the template or dimensions supplied for your exact job. Do not assume one bleed measurement works for every business card, flyer, sticker or banner.
  • Keep phone numbers, email addresses, QR codes and logos comfortably inside the safe area.
  • Extend background colours and photos to the bleed edge where the supplied template requires it.
  • Keep trim, fold and crop guides on a separate non-printing layer or remove them from the final artwork as instructed.

5. Export a clean print file

A print-ready PDF is usually a dependable format for finished artwork because it can preserve page size, text and placed images. Use a print-quality export setting and embed fonts where the application supports it. Converting text to outlines can also prevent font substitution, but keep an editable copy of the original design for later changes.

  • Check the PDF page size and orientation before sending it.
  • Make sure images are embedded rather than missing external links.
  • Review transparency, shadows and fine lines at a suitable zoom level.
  • Keep a copy of the editable source file in case changes are needed.

6. Proofread and approve the final layout

Read every name, phone number, price, web address and date carefully. Scan QR codes with a phone, check both sides of two-sided artwork, and confirm that the final quantity and product options match your order.

Before production, confirm the final artwork and order specifications with the Kazisafii team. If you need help preparing the design, share the logo, images, exact wording and finished size so the team can discuss the available assistance.

Quick print-file checklist

  • Finished size and orientation confirmed.
  • Images are suitable resolution for the final print size.
  • Logos are supplied in vector format where possible.
  • Colour has been reviewed with print expectations in mind.
  • Bleed and safe margins follow the product template.
  • Fonts and placed images are embedded or supplied correctly.
  • Spelling, phone numbers, QR codes and dates have been checked.
  • Final proof and product specifications are approved before production.
